# 0005: The window shell and the design tokens

Date: 2026-11-19. Status: accepted. Task 7 of `docs/plans/prismforge-plan-2-experience.md`.

## Context

The owner used PrismForge for the first time and said that the navigation bar needs work. The audit (`docs/research/ux-audit.md`) measured the old top bar: controls overlap at 920 px and, with unsaved changes, at the default width of 1200 px (N1). Navigation items appeared, disappeared and were renamed with the connection state (N2). Global state was spread over up to five stacked strips (N5, G1). The Diagnostics drawer covered the header and the focused control (G2, A2). Input borders and the current-item cue failed the 3:1 contrast rule (section 6.1). There were no tokens beyond five greys (section 5).

The code is in `apps/desktop/src/lib/shell` (rail, status strip, chips, notice slot, keyboard, icons), `apps/desktop/src/lib/tokens.css`, `apps/desktop/src/lib/Notice.svelte` and `apps/desktop/src/App.svelte`.

## Decisions

1. **A left rail for the areas and one status strip for state.** This is option C of the audit (section 2.6), and decision 1 of plan 2. Microsoft recommends left navigation for five to ten areas, and the plan has seven (`ux-research.md` section 1). The rail holds only navigation. The strip sits at the top of the page, where the eye rests, and keeps its words at every width.
2. **The rail never changes with the state.** Devices, Effects and Profiles are in the main group; Diagnostics and Settings are at the foot, Settings last. The same items stay in the same order when PrismForge is connected, disconnected, retrying or in demo mode (`ux-research.md` section 1, Shneiderman). An area that needs a connection shows its own empty state with the way to connect. The current area stays marked after a disconnect.
3. **Two rail widths, labels always shown.** 184 px from 1100 px up; 72 px below, and also while Diagnostics docks below 1400 px, with the icon above an 11 px label. This keeps 588 px for the page at 920 px beside a 260 px list (audit 2.6). The research guide suggests a wide rail down to 920 px; the audit's measurement wins, and labels stay, so icon-only navigation never happens.
4. **The current area has three cues.** A filled background, a 3 px accent bar (7.98:1 on the rail) and a bold label. The bar and the weight do not depend on colour. The button has `aria-current="page"`.
5. **The connection form lives on Devices.** It is the not-connected state of Devices, the first area, so the first run opens on it. The connection chip's panel has "Connect to OpenRGB", which opens Devices and puts focus in the Host field, and "Start demo mode". Effects without a connection shows the same two buttons. A form hidden behind a chip would make the first step harder to find.
6. **Global state and its actions sit in the strip.** The connection chip shows the state word and the server ("OpenRGB 127.0.0.1:6742" or "Demo backend"); its panel gives the reason in words and the actions of that state: Rescan devices and Disconnect, Stop retrying, Disconnect while connecting, or Connect and Start demo mode. When closing the window keeps PrismForge running, the panel says so. The profile chip shows the active profile and its unsaved changes ("unknown" when Rust reports `null`), "Applying Evening" during an apply, and the profile set to apply on connect when none is active; its panel has Save changes and Open Profiles. Disconnect and Stop retrying sit below a line, apart from Rescan, in the problem colour and with their consequence ("Ends the connection and stops every PrismForge effect."). This answers the audit's finding about Disconnect beside Rescan (N6). A rescan that is still running leaves Rescan focusable with `aria-disabled` and its reason. In demo mode the panel says "Demo mode: these devices are simulated. Nothing is sent to hardware." Retrying has its own waiting tone and clock icon, not the problem tone.
7. **Panels follow the WAI-ARIA disclosure pattern.** The chip is a button with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`. Opening moves focus to the first action, or to the panel itself when its only action ends the session (Stop retrying, Disconnect while connecting), so two presses of Enter cannot end it. Arrow keys, Home and End move between actions; Escape closes and returns focus to the chip; a click outside or focus leaving closes it. Escape in a panel does not also close Diagnostics. A panel moves left to stay inside the window, never starts left of an 8 px margin, and scrolls when the window is short.
8. **Demo mode is marked on every screen.** A "Demo mode" badge with a stripe pattern and the words "simulated devices", right after the connection chip, plus a 3 px striped edge under the strip, replace the 39 px banner. The connection panel says the full sentence. The marker never appears for OpenRGB.
9. **One notice slot.** Command problems, a failed close or quit, connection problems, repaired files, the tray stop report and the apply-on-connect result appear in one place under the strip. They are ordered by tone (problem, caution, waiting, information), then newest first, so a new problem goes above a standing connection warning. With more than one, only the first shows, with "Show all N notices". A notice that arrives behind the count is announced: politely (`role=status` "New notices"), or assertively when it is a problem, once on arrival and not on later re-renders. Each notice that the data allows can be dismissed; Dismiss is the acknowledgement. A connection notice has no Dismiss: it stays until the connection changes. The slot can take focus, so F6 reaches it also when it has no button. Long lists (the apply report, a tray stop that stopped everything) are behind "Details".
10. **Diagnostics docks only where the page keeps its width.** The two-pane screens need a page of about 790 px before a pane scrolls sideways (measured in Task 7). When Diagnostics is open and docked below 1400 px, the rail turns compact (72 px) and the drawer is 320 px wide, so the page keeps 1200 − 72 − 320 = 808 px at the default window. From 1400 px the rail stays wide and the drawer is 400 px: 1400 − 184 − 400 = 816 px. Below 1182 px (72 + 320 + 790) the drawer covers the page, and the page becomes `inert`. The notices stay visible and live above a covering drawer, so a problem that arrives is seen and announced. Measured in the preview with Diagnostics open and the widest content selected (Devices and Effects on the demo motherboard, Profiles on a profile with missing devices, Settings): at 1200 × 780 the rail is 72 px, the page 808 px and the drawer 320 px; at 1400 × 900 they are 184, 816 and 400 px; at 1100 × 700 the drawer covers the page (916 px) and the page is inert. No element of the page scrolls sideways at 808 or 816 px. One rule keeps anything from running under a covering drawer: `AppState.uncover()` closes it before every navigation, every strip panel action and every modal dialog (the dialog count in `lib/overlays.svelte.ts`), and an inert layer is lifted while a dialog is open. Escape closes Diagnostics only when focus is inside it.
11. **Keyboard.** The shortcuts follow the plan's final rail order now, so nobody has to relearn them later: Ctrl+1 Devices, Ctrl+2 Effects, Ctrl+4 Profiles, Ctrl+, Settings (Command on macOS). Ctrl+3 (Setup) and Ctrl+5 (Automation) do nothing and are not shown until those areas exist. The digit keys match the key position (`event.code`, Digit1 and so on) and fall back to the character, so layouts where digits need Shift work. Ctrl+, matches the comma character only, so Ctrl+W on a Dvorak layout does not open Settings. They do nothing while focus is in a field or a dialog is open. F6 and Shift+F6 move between the rail, the strip, the notices, the page and Diagnostics, and do nothing while a dialog is open. "Skip to content" is the first Tab stop. The landmarks are `nav` "Main", the "Status" region, the "Notices" region and `main`.
12. **Design tokens in one file.** `src/lib/tokens.css` holds the roles of the audit's section 5.3: surfaces, lines (`--line-subtle` for dividers, `--line-control` for control boundaries), text (`--text`, `--text-dim`, `--text-faint`), accent and focus, state colours with their surfaces and lines, demo colours, the spectrum; spacing on a 4 px base; five type sizes plus an 11 px size for compact rail labels; three weights; radii; three shadows; two durations and one easing; four layers; control, rail and drawer sizes (`--drawer-w-compact`, 320 px, was added in Task 8 for the docked drawer at the default window). Buttons, inputs, the switch, the dialog and the notice use them. Component styles use the same values by token. `--text-faint` moved to #8b91a1 and controls got `--line-control`, which clear the contrast failures of section 6.1. Button text no longer inherits a dim colour.
13. **Icons are PrismForge's own.** Five line icons on a 20 px grid with a 1.6 px round stroke, drawn for this project in `lib/shell/Icon.svelte`: a module with three LEDs (Devices), a prism that splits light (Effects), the hexagon of the mark with lines (Profiles), a trace in a frame (Diagnostics) and three sliders (Settings). Every icon has a visible label. No icon library is used.
14. **Long text: one true line, the rest behind Details.** A screen shows one short, true sentence where the action is. The full explanation sits in a `Details` disclosure (`lib/Details.svelte`, a native `details` and `summary`, so it opens with Enter and Space and says whether it is open). Every statement that was there for honesty stays, word for word where a test guards it. A disclosure is closed by default. **A statement about a hardware write, a restore limit or data that leaves the computer is never only inside a collapsed disclosure before the user acts.** Reference text may be collapsed; warnings may not. So the connection form says in a visible line when connecting applies a marked profile (from the start profile that Rust publishes after every command that can change it: mark, unmark, rename, delete, import, and the start and end of a session), How identify works is open until the first identify, and Diagnostics says what Copy as text holds. Used for the connection prerequisites, Identify, the Effects lifecycle, hardware modes, another program, the hand-over, settings help and the text about lighting changes without a click.
15. **One selection language.** A selected row in any list (devices, targets, profiles) and a chosen effect card use the rail's cue: the `--surface-3` fill, a 3 px `--accent` bar on the left and a heavier name. The rule is the shared `.pick` class in `app.css`; it reads `aria-current="true"`, `aria-checked="true"` or a checked radio inside. The spectrum bar in lists is gone. Lists are one Tab stop; the arrow keys, Home and End move and select.
16. **Marks are not buttons.** A state mark (`.tag`) is a small dot and a word, with no border and no fill, so it cannot be mistaken for a button: "Effect running", "Left to OpenRGB Effects plugin", "2 not found today", "Identical device". Its words carry the meaning; the colour only repeats it.
17. **Forced colours.** Under `forced-colors: active` (Windows contrast themes) the current-item bar and the selected row edge use `Highlight`, the switch thumb uses `CanvasText` and, when on, `Highlight`, the demo badge gets a dashed `CanvasText` border, the tone icons use `CanvasText`, and the checked close choice in Settings gets a `Highlight` border and a `Highlight` dot, while focus on it is a separate 3 px `CanvasText` ring with an offset. Focus rings are outlines, which the system keeps. The effect preview, the colour strips and the colour swatches set `forced-color-adjust: none`, because their colour is the content.
18. **Screens.** Two-pane screens share `.two-pane`: a 240–300 px list and a page that fills the rest and scrolls; an inner column caps the line length at 880–920 px. Below 760 px wide (200 % text) the list sits above the page. On Effects a command bar at the top of the page holds only the target, Apply effect, Stop effect, the edit mode (Live, Draft or Off, as a word and a coloured edge) and the output line. It never grows with results, failures, disclosures or notices: those sit in the column that scrolls below it. Only its own mode sentence can wrap to another line (152 px at 920 × 600 for a one-line mode, about 170 px for the zone-effect sentence). A result scrolls into view only as the direct result of an Apply effect or Stop effect press for the target on screen, and only when it is out of view; mounting, switching targets, returning to Effects, moving through the target list and live edits never scroll, so the control in use stays where it is. The page says once that effects run while PrismForge runs and is connected. On a whole-device target the mode names a zone effect that runs, what Apply replaces and what Stop stops. When the window is under 520 px tall the bar scrolls with the page.

19. **Context menus are shortcuts.** Task 8b added them at the owner's request ("an appropriate context menu on all screens where applicable"). The code is `lib/shell/menu.svelte.ts` (the model, the open menu and the `contextMenu` action), `lib/shell/ContextMenu.svelte` (the one menu on screen, mounted once in `App.svelte`), `lib/shell/mirrors.ts` (the registry) and one menu model per screen: `lib/shell/stripMenus.ts`, `features/devices/menu.ts`, `features/effects/menu.ts`, `features/profiles/menu.ts` and `features/diagnostics/menu.ts`.
    - **Where they are.** The connection chip: Rescan devices, Connect to OpenRGB, Start demo mode, Disconnect or Stop retrying, as its panel offers in each state. The profile chip: Save changes to <name> and Open Profiles. A device row: Set lighting, Identify device, Rename device, Use the reported name. A zone row: Identify zone, Rename zone, Use the reported name. An Effects target row: Choose this target, Stop effect (only while an effect runs on it), Hand back to PrismForge (only while another program holds the device), Hardware modes…, Leave this device to another program…. An effect card: Choose this effect, Apply to the target. A profile row: Apply profile, Save changes to <name> (the active profile only), Rename, Duplicate, Export…, Apply this profile when PrismForge connects (a checkbox item), Delete. A Diagnostics section: Copy section, Copy as text. Settings, the connection form, the rail and the notices have none: Settings and the form are single controls with their consequence beside them, so a menu would repeat one button; the rail and the notices act on a single press already.
    - **Never the only way.** Every item names, by id, the visible control it mirrors, and that control carries `data-mirror` with the same id. The item type only accepts an id from `MIRRORS`, and `lib/shell/mirrors.test.ts` opens every menu on every screen and fails when an item has no marked control on the screen (or behind a disclosure whose summary is), or when the registry holds an id no menu offers. Diagnostics got a visible Copy section button in each section for this reason. Copy name on Devices is left out: no button copies a name.
    - **Same call, same reason, same confirmation.** An item calls the method its button calls (`AppState`, `EffectsState`, `ProfilesState`, the name editor), so the apply lock, holds, Live and Draft, ownership and `Busy` stay as they are. No Tauri command was added. A disabled item shows the button's reason. Every item on a row that is not on screen first selects it (the device, the target, the profile), so the result shows where the button would show it. Rename, Export… and Delete on a profile, and Rename on a device or zone, open the same editor or dialog as the button; a dialog opened from a menu returns focus to the row. Such a request is dropped when another row is chosen before the profile's details arrive, or when they cannot be read, so it never opens later without a click. Hardware modes… and Leave this device to another program… only open their disclosure and focus it; they send nothing.
    - **Truthful labels.** Item text is the button's text ("Identify device", "Stop effect", "Hand back to PrismForge", "Apply this profile when PrismForge connects", "Save changes to Evening" on the profile, its row and the profile chip), and "Apply to Demo Graphics Card" names the target on screen. An ellipsis means more input follows, not a confirmation, and button and item agree: "Export…" (a privacy note, then a file picker) and "Save current lighting…" have one; "Delete" (a confirmation only) has none.
    - **Destructive items last.** Disconnect, Stop retrying, Leave this device to another program… and Delete sit after a separator in the problem colour; the model sorts them there (`danger: true`). Each keeps its button's confirmation; Disconnect and Stop retrying have none, as in the panel. **Focus never rests on a destructive item by itself:** an open, a rebuild and every focus fallback go to a non-destructive item, and when a menu has only destructive items (or asks for it, as the connection menu does while it can only end the session) the menu box holds focus, as the panel does, so Shift+F10 and Enter cannot end anything. Only the user's own arrow, letter or pointer moves focus onto a destructive item.
    - **Keyboard.** A secondary click, Shift+F10 and the Menu key open it. From the keyboard it opens at the focused item, below it or above when there is no room. It follows the WAI-ARIA menu pattern: `role="menu"`, `menuitem` and `menuitemcheckbox`; focus goes to the first enabled item; Up and Down wrap; Home and End; a letter moves to the next item that starts with it; Enter and Space run the item; Escape and Tab close the menu and return focus to the item it came from. Disabled items stay focusable with `aria-disabled` and their reason as the description. Escape in a menu does not close Diagnostics. A row that has no menu of its own, such as a zone row, opens from any button in it. Every control that opens a menu from the keyboard carries `aria-keyshortcuts="Shift+F10"`, added to any shortcut it already announces: the row or chip itself, or, where the row cannot take focus (a zone row, an effect card, a Diagnostics section), the buttons, radio and Copy section inside it, also those that appear later. Text fields get none, because there Shift+F10 keeps the platform menu. `aria-haspopup` stays off, since the main action of a row is selection.
    - **Overlays.** A menu never opens from inside an `inert` layer or behind a modal dialog. `overlays.popover` names the one open popover or menu, so a menu closes a chip panel and a panel closes a menu. A strip menu item runs `AppState.uncover()` first, like its panel. An open menu is built again from the state it reads whenever that state changes, so a reason or a label never goes stale; focus stays on the same item while it exists, and the menu closes when it would be empty or its item leaves the page. When the focused item turns off, the menu's own polite status region says so once ("Rescan devices is now off." and the reason); later rebuilds do not repeat it. A click outside or focus moving to another element closes it. Switching to another window does not close an open menu (it did before Task 8b fix round 1): the WebView keeps focus on the item, and the menu is still there on return. A resize places it again inside the window, with an 8 px margin, after the layout has followed. A scroll of the window or of the pane that holds its item moves it by as much as the item moved, and closes it once the item is out of view. Menus are 200 to 320 px wide (`--menu-w-min`, `--menu-w-max`) and scroll when the window is short.
    - **Forced colours.** The menu border and separator use `CanvasText`, a disabled item `GrayText`, the focused item a `Highlight` outline and a checked box `Highlight`.
20. **The WebView's own menu is suppressed in production.** WebView2 shows Reload, Back, Save as and Print on a secondary click, and Inspect where developer tools are on. Outside text fields these do nothing useful in PrismForge and Reload drops the window's state. `lib/shell/nativeMenu.ts` listens in the capture phase on the window, so it sees every `contextmenu` event, also one that a handler stops, and decides per event: in a text input, a textarea or editable content the platform menu stays (Cut, Copy, Paste, Select All); elsewhere a production build prevents it, and a development build lets it show wherever PrismForge opened no menu of its own (an app menu prevents it itself), so developers can still inspect. When Diagnostics cannot reach the clipboard it says to select the values and press Ctrl+C (Command-C on macOS). Text outside fields can still be copied with Ctrl+C. `lib/shell/nativeMenu.test.ts` covers both builds.

21. **Selecting several targets (Task 9).** The Effects target list is a list box with `aria-multiselectable`; every device and zone is an option with a visible check mark, and the whole-device option of a device with some zones checked shows a mixed mark and the description "Some zones selected". Focus and selection are apart: the arrow keys, Home and End move focus only; Space toggles the focused target; Shift with an arrow adds the next target; Shift+Space adds the range from the last toggled target; Ctrl+A (Command-A) selects every target that can take an effect; Enter selects only the focused target. With the pointer, a click selects only that target, a click on the check mark or a Ctrl-click toggles it, and a Shift-click adds a range. "Select all" and "Clear" above the list and the context menu are the visible equivalents of the modifier keys. Checking a whole device covers its zones; unchecking one zone leaves the others checked. Zones checked one by one stay zone targets, also when every zone is checked, so each zone runs the whole effect on its own LEDs; the device row then shows a full check mark with the description "Every zone selected on its own", while its own option stays unselected. Checking the device row itself selects the whole-device target. The bar names the difference: "3 zones of Demo Motherboard" against "Demo Motherboard (whole device)". A target that cannot take an effect (an identical twin, unavailable, left to another program, no Direct Mode, no LEDs) states why, has a dashed mark and stays out of a selection of several; a click still opens it alone, so its hardware modes and Hand back stay reachable. A line under the header counts the selection ("3 targets selected"). One target selected is the single-target page as before; several open a bar that names them ("Apply to 3 targets", "Stop on 3 targets") and a result list with one row per target. "Retry 1 target" resends only the targets a second try can reach (a refused or lost request, an identify, a device missing for now), never one that would be refused again (no Direct Mode, left to another program, unknown or identical device, zone out of range). When the selected targets run the chosen effect with different values, each such setting says "Mixed", Live stays off, and the bar says "Some settings differ between the targets, marked Mixed. Apply sends the values shown to all 3 targets."; changing a Mixed setting clears its mark. "Stop all PrismForge effects" sits at the foot of the target list. Groups sit below the targets; a group's line says how many members are not here today without opening anything.
22. **Two preview states.** The stage has Draft preview and Live output as two pressed buttons. Draft: the badge "Draft", the mark ◇, a dashed frame and the line "The pattern only. PrismForge sends nothing for it." Live: the badge "Live", the mark ●, a solid frame in the ok colour and "What PrismForge sends now. PrismForge cannot see the device, so this is not a reading of the lights." (in demo mode: "…to the demo backend now. Nothing reaches hardware."). Neither state relies on colour alone. The live view asks Rust for frames only while it is shown, the preview open and the window and document visible. Reduced motion keeps the draft at one still frame and the live view at a new still frame every 2 seconds.
23. **Flash cap.** Both views pass every frame through one limiter (`features/effects/flash.ts`) that follows each LED: it holds back a frame that would give a tenth or more of the LEDs on the stage a fourth flash within a second, so a fast rainbow wave or two halves that swap are caught even when the row's mean stays flat. The stage then says so. It applies to the views on the screen only, not to the physical lights; see `0003-effects-engine.md`, Preview 7.
24. **Menu items for several targets and groups.** A target row adds "Add to group…" (it keeps the selection when the row is part of it, and otherwise selects only that row first), mirrored by "Add selection to a group…" below the groups. A group row has Select, "Apply Rainbow wave to group" while an effect is chosen (it sends the chosen effect and values, never what the members run), Rename… and, apart and last, Delete…; Everything has Select and Apply only. Rename, Delete and Add open the same dialogs as their buttons; Delete keeps its confirmation, and its dialog focuses "Keep group". The target row's first item is now "Select only this target".

## How later tasks add menu items

1. Add the id and a description of its visible control to `MIRRORS` in `lib/shell/mirrors.ts`, and put `data-mirror` with that id on the control.
2. Add the item to the screen's menu model, with the same method call and the same reason as the button, and `danger: true` when it ends something.
3. Add its id to the screen's list in `lib/shell/mirrors.test.ts`, and a screen test that the item calls the same command as its button.

Task 9 added the group and Add to group items this way (decision 24). Setup and Automation get a model file in their feature folder and the `contextMenu` action on their rows. No item is added before its button exists.

## How new areas are added

Setup and Automation are not in the rail yet; the plan forbids placeholder areas. Each one is added in three steps:

1. Add its `Screen` value in `lib/state/app.svelte.ts` and its page in `App.svelte`.
2. Add one entry to `MAIN_AREAS` in `lib/shell/areas.ts`, in the plan's order: Devices, Effects, Setup, Profiles, Automation, with its reserved key (3 for Setup, 5 for Automation). The rail and the tests that compare the rail with the area list follow.
3. Draw its icon in `lib/shell/Icon.svelte`.

No shortcut changes when they arrive. The compact rail has room for both: seven items of about 52 px fit in 600 px. At 300 px high (600 px at 200 %) the rail drops the brand and tightens its items, and it never shows a scrollbar, so no label is cut.

## Not verified

- Context menus in the native window: WebView2's own secondary-click and Menu-key events, whether Chromium sends `contextmenu` for a disabled button (if it sends none, its own menu may show there), the suppression of its menu, the Shift+F10 path on Windows, and a screen reader announcing the menu and the disabled reasons. The focused item is not drawn in the preview screenshots, for the same reason as the focus rings.
- Native window chrome, minimum size and resize in Tauri.
- WebView2 rendering on Windows: Segoe UI weights, scrollbars and forced colours.
- A screen reader pass with Narrator, NVDA or VoiceOver. Announcements are checked from the markup only.
- Focus rings in the screenshots: the preview tab does not hold system focus, so `:focus-visible` is not drawn there.
- The macOS app menu changes made with this task (View and the Window menu id); Tauri's mock runtime has no native menu.
